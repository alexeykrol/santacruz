# Title: Chicago's Most Successful Screenwriter Takes to TV

Steve Conrad Interview — Influences & Patriot

"Premium TV is a writer's medium now. I wanted to dig into long-form storytelling."

## Influences Shaping Conrad's Work

**George Carlin's FM & AM**
Conrad's mother raised him Catholic, but his father gave him Carlin's comedy album. "I listened to Carlin make fun of all these things I was taught you can't make fun of." This early exposure challenged his upbringing and opened his perspective beyond parental authority.

**Lucille Ball**
Ball represented a different kind of beauty to young Conrad—one prioritizing humor over appearance. "I would race home to watch this beautiful lady not care about being beautiful, but about being funny."

**The Kids in the Hall**
The comedy troupe's work resonated as validation for creative play with friends, suggesting that improvisation could become something meaningful.

**Rankin/Bass's Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer**
Conrad describes the stop-motion holiday special as psychedelic viewing for 1980s children, noting its surprisingly dark undertones.

**Uncle Tupelo**
Jeff Tweedy's band represented brilliant music that rejected commercial radio expectations, paralleling how Kids in the Hall created television that didn't seek mainstream success.

**Tim Conrad (His Older Brother)**
Following parental divorce, his brother's adult responsibilities gave Conrad freedom to pursue music and movies during formative years.

**Charlie Chaplin**
Before genre conventions existed, Chaplin created multifaceted films unrestricted by categorical rules.

**Richard Ford's Independence Day**
This novel established Conrad's appreciation for immersive reading beyond academic study.

**George Saunders**
The author's short stories cultivate compassion for overlooked characters readers might dismiss.
